Open Globe Injury

ED framing: open globe is a vision-threatening trauma diagnosis. When suspicion becomes meaningful, stop the exam, avoid pressure, protect the eye, and get ophthalmology/OR capability moving.

Think open globe
  • High-speed metal, grinder, drill, lawn mower, explosion, BB/pellet.
  • Sharp stick, glass, knife, toy, nail, bungee cord, or prior ocular surgery scar.
  • Normal visual acuity does not exclude a small sealed laceration.
Red flags
  • Irregular/teardrop pupil, APD, hyphema, shallow or flat anterior chamber.
  • Positive Seidel, uveal/iris/vitreous prolapse, lens dislocation, IOFB.
  • Large/360 subconjunctival hemorrhage or hemorrhagic chemosis.
Initial ED actions
  • Visual acuity if possible, then gentle inspection only.
  • Rigid shield or paper cup, head up 45 degrees, NPO.
  • Analgesia, antiemetic, tetanus, IV antibiotics, CT orbit, urgent ophthalmology.

Do Not Do

AvoidWhy it matters
No tonometry or digital pressure.Pressure can extrude intraocular contents if the globe is open.
No forceful lid opening, pressure patch, probing, or removal of a protruding foreign body.Manipulation can worsen the wound and vision loss.
No ocular ultrasound pressure if suspected rupture.Tintinalli lists suspected ruptured globe as a relative contraindication to US because of extrusion risk.
No MRI if metallic foreign body is possible.CT orbit is preferred for occult rupture and IOFB localization.
