1. Introduction & General Approach

Tintinalli Ch.188 p.1243
  • Hallucinogens distort perception (visual, auditory, tactile) and may produce dissociative or psychotic states.
  • Most exposures are recreational; complications come from secondary trauma, hyperthermia, rhabdomyolysis, and polysubstance use rather than the direct drug effect.
  • An ED-stable, well-oriented patient with a "bad trip" can usually be discharged after observation and reassurance once symptoms resolve.

Initial Assessment

  1. Vital signs, fingerstick glucose, mental-status exam.
  2. Identify the offending agent if possible; recognize polysubstance exposure.
  3. Look for sympathomimetic features (HTN, tachycardia, hyperthermia) — the strongest predictor of severe outcome.
  4. Treat agitation and hyperthermia aggressively.
  5. Decontamination: activated charcoal only for early oral ingestion of toxic doses.

Inline MCQ 1. Which of these is the strongest predictor of a severe outcome in a hallucinogen-intoxicated patient?
A. Vivid visual hallucinations
B. Mild paranoia
C. Tachycardia, hypertension, hyperthermia (sympathomimetic surge)

Answer: C. Sympathomimetic features predict morbidity (rhabdo, seizures, dysrhythmia, hyperthermia). Hallucinations alone are typically self-limited. Reference: Tintinalli p.1243.

2. Lysergic Acid Diethylamide (LSD)

Tintinalli p.1243
  • Source: semi-synthetic from ergot alkaloid.
  • Route/dose: oral, 25 µg up to 80 µg per "blotter"; effects begin 30 min after ingestion, last 8–12 h.
  • Mechanism: partial 5-HT2A receptor agonist; dopaminergic effects at higher doses.
  • Clinical: mydriasis, tachycardia, mild HTN, anxiety/euphoria, synesthesia (mixing of senses), depersonalization, perceptual distortions.
  • "Bad trip": dysphoria, panic attack, paranoid delusions — supportive care + benzodiazepines.
  • HPPD (hallucinogen-persisting perception disorder) — flashback-like visual disturbances months after use.
  • Death from LSD itself is rare; almost all fatalities are from trauma during impaired judgment.
Pearl: LSD does NOT cause significant cardiotoxicity at recreational doses. The main ED concern is psychiatric — panic, self-harm — and behavioral injury.

3. Psilocybin (Magic Mushrooms)

Tintinalli p.1243–1244
  • Source: Psilocybe species mushrooms; psilocybin → psilocin (active metabolite).
  • Mechanism: 5-HT2A agonist (similar to LSD).
  • Onset: 30 min; duration: 4–6 h.
  • Clinical: mydriasis, mild tachycardia, mild HTN, perceptual distortions; nausea/vomiting common.
  • Complications: rare hyperthermia and rhabdomyolysis; case reports of seizures.
  • Misidentification risk: people may ingest highly toxic look-alike mushrooms (e.g., Amanita species — see Ch.220 Mushroom Poisoning) — consider this if symptoms don't fit psilocybin.
Inline MCQ 2. A college student presents with abdominal pain, vomiting, and altered mental status 12 h after eating "magic mushrooms" picked in the wild. Which is the most concerning consideration?
A. Typical psilocybin trip
B. Withdrawal
C. Misidentified Amanita species causing hepatotoxicity
D. Pure caffeine intoxication
Answer: C. Onset of GI symptoms 6–12 h after ingestion of foraged mushrooms is classic for amatoxin (Amanita phalloides). Psilocybin is short-acting (4–6 h). Get LFTs, INR, coag panel and consult poison control. Reference: Tintinalli p.1244.

4. Mescaline & Peyote

Tintinalli p.1244–1245
  • Source: Lophophora williamsii (Peyote cactus), Trichocereus pachanoi (San Pedro). Used in Native American religious ceremonies.
  • Dose: 3–12 "buttons" containing 200–500 mg mescaline.
  • Onset: 1–2 h; duration: 6–12 h.
  • Clinical: mydriasis, abdominal pain, prominent nausea/vomiting, dizziness, ataxia, nystagmus.
  • Treatment: typically supportive; benzodiazepines for severe agitation.

5. MDMA ("Ecstasy")

Tintinalli p.1244 + Ch.187
  • Methylenedioxymethamphetamine — combined sympathomimetic + serotonergic effects.
  • Dose: typical 50–200 mg; onset: 30–60 min; duration: 4–6 h.
  • Clinical: empathogenic euphoria, mydriasis, bruxism, jaw tension, ataxia, dry mouth, hyperthermia.
  • Major complications:
    • Hyponatremia from SIADH + excessive water intake at parties — seizures, cerebral edema.
    • Hyperthermia — from muscle hyperactivity + dehydration.
    • Serotonin syndrome (especially if combined with other serotonergic drugs).
    • Rhabdomyolysis, dysrhythmias, hepatotoxicity.
  • Treatment: benzodiazepines, aggressive cooling, IV fluids (with caution if hyponatremia present), cyproheptadine for serotonin syndrome, dantrolene if true malignant hyperthermia features.
⚠️ The MDMA hyponatremia trap: A young patient at a rave with seizures + temp 40°C + Na 121 + cerebral edema needs 3% hypertonic saline boluses — NOT free water. Standard "rehydrate with NS" can worsen edema.

6. Bath Salts (Synthetic Cathinones)

Tintinalli p.1244
  • Examples: mephedrone, MDPV, methylone — sold under names "bath salts," "plant food."
  • Mechanism: sympathomimetic + hallucinogenic; structurally distinct from amphetamines so often missed by standard immunoassay screens — need GC/MS.
  • Clinical: agitation, tachycardia, HTN, mydriasis, diaphoresis + hallucinations, paranoia, panic.
  • Severe complications: hyperthermia, seizures, rhabdomyolysis, hyponatremia, sudden death.
  • Treatment: benzodiazepines, hydration, active cooling.

7. Phencyclidine (PCP / "Angel Dust")

Tintinalli p.1245
  • Mechanism: NMDA-receptor antagonist (dissociative anesthetic).
  • Onset: minutes; duration: 4–6 h, but psychotic features may persist days.
  • Distinctive features: small or normal-sized pupils (NOT mydriasis), nystagmus (often vertical or rotary), muscle rigidity, hypersalivation, agitation, catatonia.
  • Cycling between extreme agitation and unresponsiveness.
  • Severe: hyperthermia, rhabdomyolysis, seizures, HTN crisis, hypoglycemia, prolonged psychosis.
  • Treatment: low-stimulation environment, benzodiazepines for sedation; haloperidol can be added but caution re QT/seizure threshold.
Inline MCQ 3. Which feature most reliably distinguishes PCP intoxication from cocaine/amphetamine sympathomimetic toxidrome?
A. Tachycardia
B. Vertical/rotary nystagmus + small or normal pupils + muscle rigidity
C. Hyperthermia
D. Hallucinations
Answer: B. PCP characteristically produces nystagmus (especially vertical), normal/small pupils (vs mydriasis in stimulants), muscle rigidity, hypersalivation. Reference: Tintinalli p.1245.

8. Marijuana & Synthetic Cannabinoids

Tintinalli p.1245–1247

Marijuana (cannabis)

  • Active component: THC (Δ9-tetrahydrocannabinol). Dose 5–15 mg THC; duration 2–4 h.
  • Clinical: tachycardia, conjunctival injection, dry mouth, euphoria, increased appetite, time distortion.
  • Complications (rare): acute psychosis, panic attacks, vomiting (cannabinoid hyperemesis with chronic heavy use — relieved by hot showers, treated with capsaicin cream and haloperidol).

Synthetic Cannabinoids ("K2," "Spice," "Fake Marijuana")

  • Designed to mimic THC at cannabinoid receptors but typically much more potent.
  • Clinical: tachycardia + conjunctival injection (like marijuana) but with more severe psychosis, seizures, dysrhythmias, hyperthermia.
  • Standard urine cannabinoid screens may be negative — frequent updates to formulations evade detection.
  • Treatment: supportive; benzodiazepines for agitation/seizures.
Cannabinoid hyperemesis syndrome: chronic heavy cannabis user with cyclic vomiting, abdominal pain, relieved by hot showers. Workup negative. Treat: capsaicin cream to abdomen + haloperidol/droperidol; cannabis cessation curative.

9. Designer Alkaloids — Bromo-DragonFLY

Tintinalli p.1247
  • Bromo-benzodifuranyl-isopropylamine (Bromo-DragonFLY): phenylethylamine analog with potent 5-HT2A activity.
  • Dose: 200–800 µg.
  • Onset: may begin within 4 h; duration: 10–14 h (very long).
  • Clinical: hallucinations + sympathomimetic effects.
  • Severe complication: peripheral vasoconstriction with ischemia → necrosis and gangrene of limbs (unique among hallucinogens).
  • Sometimes mistaken for or sold as LSD; small dose may produce overdose.

10. Less Commonly Abused Hallucinogens

Tintinalli Table 188-2 p.1247
📚 Table 188-2 — Less Commonly Abused Hallucinogens (Tintinalli p.1247)
  • Salvia divinorum (salvinorin A): κ-opioid agonist; smoked or buccal — short, intense dissociative experience.
  • Toad venom / eggs (bufotoxins): cardiac glycoside-like effects — abdominal pain, vomiting, dysrhythmias mimicking digitalis toxicity. Treatment includes digoxin-specific Fab if cardiac glycoside features dominate.
  • Morning glory seeds (lysergic acid amide): mild LSD-like effects.
  • Nutmeg (myristicin): anticholinergic-like effects after large doses.
  • Datura (Jimson weed, angel's trumpet): pure anticholinergic toxidrome (mydriasis, dry skin, ↓ bowel sounds, urinary retention, tachycardia, hyperthermia, delirium). Duration up to 24–48 h.
  • Ketamine: dissociative anesthetic; sympathomimetic effects + dissociation; medical use for sedation in ED.
  • Dextromethorphan (DXM): NMDA antagonist + serotonergic; dissociative effects, nausea, vomiting; risk of serotonin syndrome with concurrent serotonergic agents.
Inline MCQ 4. A teenager presents with mydriasis, dry hot skin, urinary retention, hallucinations, and tachycardia after ingesting plant material. Which exposure is most likely?
A. LSD
B. Magic mushrooms
C. Datura (Jimson weed)
D. Marijuana
Answer: C. Pure anticholinergic toxidrome (mydriasis, dry skin, retention, tachycardia, delirium) from scopolamine/atropine in Datura. Reference: Tintinalli Table 188-2.
Inline MCQ 5. A patient ingested toad venom and presents with abdominal pain, vomiting, bradycardia, and ECG showing scooped ST segments. The most appropriate antidote is:
A. Naloxone
B. Digoxin-specific Fab fragments
C. Physostigmine

Answer: B. Bufotoxins act like cardiac glycosides → digitalis-like toxicity. Digoxin Fab fragments are the antidote. Reference: Tintinalli Table 188-2 + Ch.193 Digitalis.

11. Treatment — General Principles

Tintinalli p.1243

Sedation

  • Benzodiazepines first-line: lorazepam 1–2 mg IV q5–10min, diazepam 5 mg IV, midazolam 2–5 mg IV/IM. No fixed maximum dose — titrate to calm.
  • Antipsychotics (haloperidol, droperidol, olanzapine) second-line; risk of QT prolongation, lowered seizure threshold.
  • Ketamine (4 mg/kg IM) for severe excited delirium when benzos fail.

Hyperthermia

  • Aggressive cooling (evaporative or ice bath).
  • Sedation reduces muscle activity and heat production.
  • Antipyretics ineffective (not pyrogenic).
  • Dantrolene reserved for confirmed serotonin syndrome with marked rigidity, malignant hyperthermia, or NMS — not standard for stimulant hyperthermia.

Hypertension

  • Benzodiazepines first.
  • Phentolamine for refractory HTN.
  • Avoid pure β-blockers (theoretical "unopposed α").

Seizures

  • Benzodiazepines first; phenobarbital second; intubation + propofol/midazolam for status.
  • Check sodium (MDMA-induced hyponatremia).

Decontamination

  • Activated charcoal: only if early oral ingestion of toxic dose AND airway is protected.
  • No specific antidote for most hallucinogens.
Inline MCQ 6. Which is the most appropriate first-line agent for an agitated, hallucinating patient with a hallucinogen overdose and otherwise stable vitals?
A. Haloperidol 5 mg IM
B. Lorazepam 1–2 mg IV titrated
C. Physical restraints
D. Activated charcoal
Answer: B. Benzos are first-line for hallucinogen-induced agitation. Antipsychotics second-line; physical restraints prolonged are dangerous. Reference: Tintinalli p.1243.

12. Disposition

Tintinalli p.1243
  • Discharge: ambulatory, normal vitals, alert, no medical complications, with reliable observer for several hours after symptoms resolve.
  • Admit: persistent psychosis, severe hyperthermia, rhabdomyolysis, hyponatremia, prolonged effects (e.g., Bromo-DragonFLY 10–14 h, Datura 24–48 h, PCP psychosis days).
  • Psychiatric consult: persistent suicidal/homicidal ideation, prolonged psychosis (e.g., HPPD), substance-use referral.

⚕️ ATLS 11th — Hallucinogen-Intoxicated Trauma Patient

ATLS
  • Primary survey may be obscured by agitation/uncooperativeness — sedate to allow assessment.
  • Tachycardia and HTN may not reflect hemorrhage — be aware sympathomimetic features can mask early hypovolemic shock.
  • Rhabdomyolysis from prolonged agitation/restraints in addition to mechanical injury — early IVF + monitor urine output and CK.
  • Sedation: benzodiazepines preferred; ketamine acceptable; succinylcholine relatively contraindicated if hyperkalemia from rhabdo.
  • Imaging: low threshold for CT head with suspected trauma + altered mental status that doesn't fit purely hallucinogen toxidrome.

📗 Rosen's EM 10th — Hallucinogens

Rosen's Ch.150
  • Same overall framework as Tintinalli: sympathomimetic surveillance, supportive care + benzodiazepines, aggressive cooling for hyperthermia.
  • Rosen's emphasizes polysubstance exposure in real-world ED hallucinogen presentations — toxicology screen often misses bath salts, synthetic cannabinoids, and emerging designer drugs.
  • "Bad trip" psychiatric care: low-stimulation environment, calm verbal de-escalation BEFORE pharmacologic restraint.
  • Datura/Jimson weed: physostigmine cautiously considered for severe pure anticholinergic delirium (avoid in patients with QRS widening, asthma, or unstable cardiac disease).
  • Hallucinogen-persisting perception disorder (HPPD): may require psychiatry referral for SSRIs, anticonvulsants, or naltrexone trials.

🔑 Key Points (High-Yield)

  1. LSD and psilocybin = 5-HT₂A agonists; main risks are trauma during impaired judgment + bad trips.
  2. PCP = NMDA antagonist; vertical nystagmus, normal/small pupils, muscle rigidity distinguish from stimulants.
  3. MDMA + free water at raves → severe hyponatremia + cerebral edema; treat with 3% saline.
  4. Bath salts (cathinones) miss standard urine immunoassays — need GC/MS.
  5. Synthetic cannabinoids (K2/spice) are far more potent than marijuana — severe seizures, dysrhythmias, hyperthermia.
  6. Bromo-DragonFLY → unique limb ischemia/gangrene; effects last 10–14 h.
  7. Datura = pure anticholinergic toxidrome; physostigmine cautiously for severe delirium.
  8. Toad venom = digitalis-like toxicity → use digoxin Fab.
  9. Cannabinoid hyperemesis in chronic users — capsaicin + haloperidol + cessation.
  10. Foraged mushrooms with delayed GI symptoms (>6 h) → suspect amatoxin, get LFTs.
  11. Treatment paradigm: benzo first-line, cool aggressively, treat agitation early, low-stim environment.
  12. Most fatalities are from secondary trauma / hyperthermia / rhabdo / hyponatremia — not direct drug toxicity.
