Renal and Genitourinary Disorders

Complications of Urologic Procedures and Devices

Urologic devices create ED problems through infection, obstruction, migration, erosion, bleeding, and mechanical failure. Identify the device first, then decide whether the problem is benign, needs replacement, or needs urgent urology.

Board trap: Do not treat catheter bacteriuria alone as infection; symptoms, systemic signs, and device source control decide management.

Complications of Urologic Procedures and Devices

COMPLICATIONS OF URINARY CATHETERS

Use urinary catheters sparingly. Indwelling urethral and suprapubic catheters share infectious, obstructive, leakage, and traumatic complications.

INFECTION

CAUTI is common, but bacteriuria and pyuria are frequent colonization findings, especially with long-term catheters. Diagnose symptomatic infection by fever, rigors, altered mental status, malaise, flank/pelvic discomfort, hematuria, pelvic pain, autonomic dysreflexia, or catheter-specific symptoms plus culture support.

CAUTI symptoms and diagnosis requirements.

Remove the catheter if feasible or replace it before culture/treatment when symptomatic infection is suspected. Treat symptomatic CAUTI with empiric antibiotics guided by local resistance, then tailor to culture.

CATHETER OBSTRUCTION AND LEAKAGE

Obstruction can result from kinking, encrustation, clots, malposition, bladder spasm, or debris. Leakage around a catheter suggests obstruction, bladder spasm, or incorrect catheter/balloon position rather than automatic UTI.

NONDEFLATION OF FOLEY RETENTION BALLOON

Nondeflating balloons require stepwise troubleshooting. Avoid forceful removal; consider cutting the inflation port, guidewire/needle techniques by trained clinicians, or urology.

TRAUMATIC COMPLICATIONS

False passage, urethral trauma, bleeding, bladder injury, and retained fragments can follow difficult placement or removal. Blood at the meatus or severe pain after catheterization deserves stop-and-consult thinking.

COMPLICATIONS OF PERCUTANEOUS NEPHROSTOMY AND NEPHROLITHOTOMY

Nephrostomy drains obstructed collecting systems; nephrolithotomy removes renal calculi. ED complications include hemorrhage, infection/pyelonephritis/sepsis, tube dislodgement, blockage, leak, retained fragments, and adjacent organ injury. CT helps when the diagnosis is unclear.

URINARY DIVERSION AND ORTHOTOPIC BLADDER SUBSTITUTION

Diversions and bladder substitutes often have colonized urine. Diagnose infection from symptoms and systemic findings, not colonization alone. Watch for obstruction, stones, pyelonephritis, renal failure, and dehydration.

LITHOTRIPSY

Lithotripsy commonly causes colicky pain, gross hematuria, bruising, and steinstrasse; serious problems include hematoma, pyelonephritis/sepsis, bowel injury, ureteric perforation, and psoas abscess. Evaluate with CBC, chemistry, UA/culture, and imaging when pain, fever, flank mass, instability, or persistent hematuria is present.

COMPLICATIONS OF URETERAL STENTS

Stents maintain ureteral patency after stones, strictures, trauma, malignancy, retroperitoneal fibrosis, or GU surgery. Complications are infection, irritative symptoms, hematuria, migration, obstruction, encrustation, fragmentation, erosion, and rarely vascular fistula.

Simple irritative symptoms are common, but fever, severe flank pain, gross hematuria, or systemic illness needs evaluation for migration, obstruction, infection, or erosion. Systemic stent infection needs IV antibiotics, imaging, and urology.

PROSTATE SURGICAL PROCEDURES

After TURP or prostate procedures, assess retention, bleeding/clots, infection, and electrolyte abnormalities such as hyponatremia. Significant hematuria or retention may need irrigation and urology.

COMPLICATIONS OF ARTIFICIAL URINARY SPHINCTERS

Artificial urinary sphincters treat incontinence using cuff, pump, and reservoir. Infection may be early skin flora or late urinary flora; erosion and mechanical failure cause pain, erythema, discharge, retention, or recurrent incontinence.

COMPLICATIONS OF TREATMENTS FOR ERECTILE DYSFUNCTION

PDE5 inhibitors interact dangerously with nitrates. Vacuum devices can cause pain, bruising, hematoma, or skin injury. Penile injections/prostheses can cause priapism, infection, erosion, migration, purulent discharge, and postoperative retention.

VASECTOMY

Postvasectomy complications include wound infection, hematoma, epididymitis, sperm granuloma, chronic pain, and rare Fournier-type infection. Treat epididymitis with support, ice, analgesia, and antibiotics when infectious features suggest it.

ADULT CIRCUMCISION

Adult circumcision complications include bleeding, infection, pain, edema, and wound dehiscence. Local wound care and analgesia are common; consult urology for significant bleeding, infection, device/ring problems, or ischemic concern.
