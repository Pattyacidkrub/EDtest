Toxicology Chapter 181

Lithium

Lithium toxicity is a kinetics chapter: the number matters, but exposure pattern, renal function, volume status, neurologic exam, and serial trend decide management.

Board frame: acute, chronic, and acute-on-chronic lithium poisoning are not interpreted the same way.

Pharmacokinetics

Lithium is a small monovalent cation with minimal protein binding and renal elimination. Volume depletion, renal impairment, NSAIDs, ACE inhibitors, ARBs, and thiazide diuretics can reduce clearance and raise levels. Chronic toxicity is dangerous because lithium has already distributed into the CNS.

Acute ingestions may have high early serum levels before CNS toxicity evolves, while sustained-release preparations can continue absorbing. Serial levels and repeated neurologic exams are mandatory when the timing or formulation is uncertain.

Clinical Features

GI symptoms such as nausea, vomiting, and diarrhea often occur early, but the dangerous findings are neurologic: coarse tremor, ataxia, dysarthria, confusion, myoclonus, seizures, and coma. Chronic toxicity may present subtly with weakness, falls, delirium, or worsening tremor.

Diagnosis

Diagnosis uses the history, exposure pattern, symptoms, renal function, electrolytes, and serial lithium concentrations. A single level is never enough when the ingestion is acute, sustained-release, or acute-on-chronic. Check creatinine, sodium, glucose, ECG when indicated, pregnancy status when relevant, and coingestants if the story is unclear.

Do not be falsely reassured by a moderate level in a chronic user with confusion or ataxia.

Treatment

Stop lithium and interacting drugs, protect the airway if needed, treat seizures with benzodiazepines, and restore euvolemia with isotonic saline. Hydration supports renal elimination, but monitor for volume overload in renal or cardiac disease.

Activated charcoal does not bind lithium. Whole-bowel irrigation can be considered for selected large sustained-release ingestions if the patient is stable and the airway is protected.

Dialysis / Extracorporeal Removal

Hemodialysis is the definitive enhanced elimination tool for severe lithium poisoning because lithium is small, water soluble, and minimally protein bound. The decision is clinical: coma, seizure, severe confusion, dysrhythmia, renal failure, very high or rising levels, and chronic toxicity with neurologic impairment all lower the threshold.

Rebound can occur after dialysis as lithium redistributes from tissues, so levels and symptoms must be followed after treatment and dialysis repeated when needed.

Disposition

Admit symptomatic patients, chronic toxicity, renal impairment, rising levels, sustained-release ingestion, or anyone needing dialysis consideration. Discharge requires improving symptoms, stable renal function, reliable follow-up, and serial lithium levels trending down.
