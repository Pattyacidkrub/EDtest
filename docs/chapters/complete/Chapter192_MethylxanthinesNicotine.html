Methylxanthines and Nicotine

Theophylline danger, severe caffeine exposure, and two-phase nicotine toxicity.

MCQ 28Source images 9Crop QA PASS

Overview

Methylxanthines include caffeine, theophylline, theobromine, and pentoxifylline. Theophylline is the board-danger drug: delayed absorption, narrow therapeutic range, chronic toxicity, seizures, dysrhythmias, hypokalemia, multidose charcoal, and hemodialysis all affect ED management.

Nicotine has an early stimulation phase and a delayed receptor-inactivation phase. Early toxicity causes GI/autonomic findings; severe delayed toxicity causes bradycardia, hypotension, hypoventilation, seizure, and coma.

Methylxanthines: Products and Kinetics

Routine beverages are common, but concentrated caffeine powder/liquid and theophylline sustained-release preparations create serious risk. Clearance changes from illness, age, smoking changes, or drug interactions can turn chronic theophylline therapy toxic.

Methylxanthine Toxicity

Mechanisms include adenosine antagonism, catecholamine excess, and phosphodiesterase inhibition. The bedside pattern is vomiting, tremor/agitation, seizure, tachydysrhythmia, hypotension, hyperglycemia, metabolic acidosis, and hypokalemia.

Diagnosis

Caffeine levels are rarely rapidly available, so severe caffeine toxicity is clinical. Theophylline levels are useful but must be serially trended and interpreted with acute vs chronic context. Chronic toxicity may be severe at lower serum levels because tissue burden and comorbidities dominate.

Trend

ECG, potassium, glucose, creatinine, lactate/acid-base status, CK when severe, and serial theophylline levels when relevant.

Do not miss

Sustained-release products can peak late, and chronic users can crash after small clearance changes.

Methylxanthine Treatment

Treatment starts with resuscitation, ECG monitoring, electrolyte correction, antiemetics, and early seizure control. Benzodiazepines are first-line; phenobarbital is next when refractory. Phenytoin is not useful.

Activated charcoal is used when safe, and multidose activated charcoal is core for toxic or potentially toxic theophylline ingestion. Hemodialysis is effective for severe theophylline toxicity and may help massive caffeine or pentoxifylline exposure.

Nicotine

Nicotine exposures include tobacco, e-cigarette liquid, gum, patches, pesticides, and dermal tobacco handling. Remove dermal contamination and wash skin. Patch ingestion needs longer observation.

Immediate toxicity is stimulation; delayed toxicity is receptor inactivation. Treat vomiting, seizures, hypotension, and hypoventilation supportively.
