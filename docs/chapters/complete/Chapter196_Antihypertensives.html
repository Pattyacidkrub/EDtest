Toxicology Chapter 196

Antihypertensive Poisoning

Most antihypertensive overdoses outside β-blockers and calcium channel blockers are not as catastrophically cardiotoxic, but they are exam-rich because each class creates a different failure pattern: volume depletion, bradycardic coma, bradykinin angioedema, or reflex tachycardia with ischemia.

Board lens: start with the same resuscitation frame every time: airway if needed, IV access, cardiac monitor, ECG, crystalloid for hypotension, and a direct-acting vasopressor such as norepinephrine when fluids fail.

Overview

What this chapter includesDiuretics, sympatholytics, ACE inhibitors, ARBs, and vasodilators. β-blockers and CCBs live in Ch.194 and Ch.195.
Default severityLife-threatening toxicity is uncommon for most isolated ingestions, so supportive care is usually enough.
First moveAirway, IV, monitor, ECG, glucose/electrolytes, and volume resuscitation before chasing rare antidotes.
Pressor choiceUse direct-acting vasopressors for refractory hypotension because depleted catecholamine stores may blunt indirect agents.

Diuretics

Diuretic toxicity is usually volume + electrolytes, not primary poison-induced shock. The ECG tells you whether the electrolyte problem is now clinically dangerous.

ThiazidesHypovolemia, hypokalemia, hypercalcemia, glucose intolerance, and hyperuricemia at higher therapeutic doses.
LoopsHypovolemia with hypokalemia, hypocalcemia, and hypomagnesemia; think prolonged QT risk.
Potassium-sparingAmiloride/triamterene and mineralocorticoid antagonists can produce hyperkalemia plus hypotension.
Interaction trapDiuretics and ACEIs can reduce lithium elimination; hypokalemia worsens digoxin/antiarrhythmic toxicity.
Treatment frame: restore plasma volume with isotonic crystalloid, then correct potassium, magnesium, calcium, and renal perfusion problems using standard electrolyte algorithms.

Sympatholytics

Sympatholytics lower blood pressure by reducing adrenergic tone. The important exam split is α1 blockers causing orthostatic vasodilation versus clonidine/imidazolines causing bradycardic CNS depression that looks opioid-like.

α1 blockersDoxazosin, prazosin, terazosin, and tamsulosin: orthostatic hypotension, especially within 30-90 minutes and after the first dose.
α1 treatmentSupine positioning, crystalloid bolus, then an α1 agonist such as phenylephrine if refractory.
Clonidine patternBradycardia + hypotension + CNS depression + miosis; apnea can occur, especially in children.
Look-alikesOxymetazoline and tetrahydrozoline ingestion can mimic clonidine because of imidazoline activity.
Do not over-treat early hypertension: clonidine may briefly cause peripheral α2-mediated hypertension before central sympatholysis dominates. If severe/prolonged, choose a short-acting agent.
Naloxone nuance: clonidine neurologic/respiratory depression has variable naloxone response. It is unlikely to help adults reliably, but it may be more useful in children.

ACE Inhibitors and ARBs

ACEI/ARB overdoses rarely produce life-threatening isolated hypotension. Their high-yield toxicity is hyperkalemia and, more importantly, bradykinin-mediated angioedema.

ACEI suffixMost end in -pril: captopril, enalapril, benazepril, lisinopril-family naming logic.
ARB suffixMost end in -artan: losartan, valsartan, candesartan, irbesartan, telmisartan.
CoughACEI cough is not dose-related, often begins within weeks, and resolves after stopping the drug; ARBs are a substitute.
Angioedema clueNo urticaria. Swelling of lips, tongue, larynx, pharynx, or vocal cords can progress over hours and persist 24+ hours.
Airway first: stridor, dyspnea, lingual edema, or oropharyngeal edema means early airway control by a skilled operator. Landmarks can disappear fast.
Medication pitfall: epinephrine, antihistamines, and corticosteroids are often given but have no proven benefit for ACEI/ARB angioedema because it is not histamine-mediated.
Rosen cross-check

Rosen's toxicology approach agrees with the practical ED frame: stabilize airway and hemodynamics first; antidote-like therapies for bradykinin angioedema are adjuncts, not substitutes for airway judgment.

Vasodilators

Vasodilator toxicity is mostly arteriolar/venous dilation, but the board loves the mechanism-specific complications: hydralazine ischemia/lupus-like syndrome, minoxidil reflex tachycardia/fluid retention, and nitroprusside cyanide/thiocyanate toxicity.

HydralazineArteriolar vasodilator, used in pregnancy. Overdose can cause hypotension, reflex tachycardia, and ischemic ST depression from demand/supply mismatch.
MinoxidilPotassium-channel opener after metabolism to minoxidil sulfate; hypotension with reflex tachycardia and occasional myocardial ischemia.
NitroprussideOnset about 30 seconds, offset about 3 minutes. Stop infusion for hypotension; supportive positioning/fluids usually suffice.
FenoldopamD1 agonist vasodilator/diuretic with short half-life; stop infusion and give fluids if hypotension occurs.
Hydralazine/minoxidil pressor choice: avoid β-agonist-heavy escalation if possible because tachycardia can worsen myocardial oxygen demand; prefer α1 activity such as phenylephrine or midodrine when fluids fail.
Nitroprusside toxicity: high-dose or prolonged infusion can generate cyanide; confusion, lactic acidosis, and cardiovascular collapse should trigger stopping the infusion and giving sodium thiosulfate.

Key Points

1. Most isolated overdoses are supportive-care casesDo not treat them like β-blocker/CCB shock unless the physiology says so.
2. Diuretics = volume + electrolytesLook for K/Mg/Ca changes and ECG consequences.
3. Clonidine can look opioid-likeMiosis, bradycardia, CNS depression, and apnea are the classic cluster.
4. A single clonidine tablet can hurt a childPediatric exploratory ingestion is high-yield.
5. ACEI angioedema is not anaphylaxisNo urticaria; airway and bradykinin pathway matter more than histamine meds.
6. Stop the culprit infusionNitroprusside and fenoldopam hypotension often improves after stopping because duration is short.
7. Nitroprusside can make cyanideThink lactic acidosis, confusion, collapse, and sodium thiosulfate.
8. Hydralazine/minoxidil tachycardia mattersVasopressors that worsen tachycardia can worsen ischemia.
