1. Introduction & Epidemiology

Tintinalli Ch.189 p.1247

Rosen vs Tintinalli: Rosen adds ED-facing nuance for salicylates while keeping the Tintinalli treatment spine.

  • Salicylates include aspirin (acetylsalicylic acid, ASA), bismuth subsalicylate (Pepto-Bismol), oil of wintergreen (methyl salicylate), and topical preparations (analgesic balms, keratolytics).
  • Toxic dose: >150 mg/kg = mild; 150–300 mg/kg = moderate; 300–500 mg/kg = severe; >500 mg/kg = potentially lethal.
  • Methyl salicylate (oil of wintergreen) is highly concentrated — 1 teaspoon (5 mL) ≈ 7 g aspirin; can be lethal in a child.
  • Two patterns: acute single ingestion (typical suicide) vs chronic/therapeutic supratherapeutic (often elderly with arthritis, more occult, higher mortality).
Pearl — methyl salicylate hidden lethality: oil of wintergreen, BenGay, and similar topicals can cause severe toxicity if ingested or absorbed through broken skin (especially in children). 5 mL methyl salicylate ≈ 21 adult aspirin tablets.

2. Pathophysiology

Tintinalli Table 189-1 p.1249
📚 Table 189-1 — Pathophysiology of Salicylate Toxicity (multiple mechanisms list) (Tintinalli p.1249)

Multiple toxic mechanisms (per Table 189-1)

  • Local gastric irritation — direct mucosal injury.
  • Reversible ototoxicity — tinnitus is one of the earliest signs.
  • Stimulation of the chemoreceptor trigger zone → nausea/vomiting.
  • Stimulation of medullary respiratory center → tachypnea + hyperpnea → primary respiratory alkalosis.
  • Stimulation of skeletal muscle metabolism → ↑ heat production.
  • Uncoupling of oxidative phosphorylation in mitochondria → metabolic acidosis (lactate from anaerobic metabolism), hyperthermia, ↑ O₂ consumption.
  • Enhancement of lipolysis → ketosis.
  • Increased vascular permeability → noncardiogenic pulmonary edema, cerebral edema.
  • Mobilization of glycogen stores → early hyperglycemia.
  • Inhibition of gluconeogenesis → late hypoglycemia (especially CNS).

Pharmacokinetics

  • Rapid absorption from stomach + small bowel; peak plasma 1–2 h (immediate-release), delayed up to 6 h with enteric-coated or large doses.
  • Therapeutic half-life 2–4 h; at toxic levels: 18–36 h (saturation kinetics).
  • Metabolism: hepatic (90%) → glycine + glucuronide conjugates → renal excretion. Saturation of glycine pathway in overdose.
  • Salicylate is a weak acid (pKa ~3); ionization shifts with pH.
  • Acidic urine + acidic plasma → more nonionized salicylate → crosses BBB and cell membranes → worse CNS toxicity.
  • Alkalinization (raising pH) → traps salicylate as ionized form in urine → renal elimination ↑ 10–20×.
Why alkalinization works: at physiologic pH, only ~3% of salicylate is non-ionized. Lowering pH (acidemia) increases non-ionized fraction → more crosses BBB → more CNS toxicity. Raising pH does the opposite — less CNS penetration + more renal excretion (ion trapping in alkaline urine).

3. Clinical Features

Tintinalli p.1249

Classic presentation in adults

  • Tinnitus, hearing loss — earliest signs (reversible ototoxicity).
  • Tachypnea + hyperpnea — respiratory alkalosis.
  • Nausea, vomiting — direct gastric irritation + CTZ stimulation.
  • Hyperthermia — uncoupling of oxidative phosphorylation.
  • Diaphoresis.
  • Volume depletion from vomiting + insensible loss + tachypnea.
  • Anion-gap metabolic acidosis — develops over hours from accumulated organic acids (lactate, ketoacids, salicylate itself).

Severe toxicity

  • Mixed acid-base disorder: respiratory alkalosis + anion-gap metabolic acidosis (classic combo in adults).
  • CNS: agitation → confusion → seizure → coma.
  • Cardiovascular: tachycardia, hypotension, dysrhythmias.
  • Pulmonary: noncardiogenic pulmonary edema (especially with chronic toxicity).
  • Renal failure (often from hypovolemia + direct toxicity).
  • Coagulopathy: hypoprothrombinemia (vitamin K-dependent factor inhibition).
  • Glucose: early hyperglycemia → late hypoglycemia in CSF (even with normal serum glucose); altered mental status warrants treatment.

Pediatric presentations

  • Children <4 years often have a different pattern: predominant metabolic acidosis rather than the mixed adult pattern.
  • Rapid progression to severe acidosis, hyperthermia, seizures.
  • Children are at greater risk of mortality at lower doses.
⚠️ Pearl — "ASA" vs the adult pattern: Adults classically show mixed respiratory alkalosis + anion-gap metabolic acidosis. Children <4 typically show pure metabolic acidosis. Loss of respiratory alkalosis (CO₂ rises) in adults is OMINOUS — patient is tiring out → impending respiratory failure.

5. Diagnosis & Workup

Tintinalli p.1250

Workup

  • Salicylate level on arrival — repeat every 1–2 h until peak/declining (delayed peak with enteric-coated or massive ingestion).
  • ABG/VBG + lactate — assess acid-base status.
  • Basic chemistry — Na, K, Cl, HCO₃, BUN, Cr, glucose.
  • CBC, coags, LFTs.
  • APAP level — common co-ingestant in suicide attempts.
  • Urine pH for monitoring alkalinization.
  • ECG.
  • Imaging: CXR if respiratory distress (suspect ARDS); CT brain if AMS to exclude alternative cause.

Salicylate level interpretation

  • Therapeutic: 10–30 mg/dL (for anti-inflammatory; lower for analgesic/antiplatelet effect).
  • Mild–moderate toxicity: 30–60 mg/dL.
  • Severe toxicity: >60 mg/dL.
  • Critical: >90 mg/dL acute / >60 mg/dL chronic — consider hemodialysis.
Pearl — Repeat the level: a single salicylate level can be falsely reassuring. Repeat every 1–2 h until you have a clear DOWNWARD trend AND clinical improvement. Enteric-coated and bezoars can produce delayed peaks 6–24 h post-ingestion.

"Triad" of mixed acid-base in salicylate toxicity

  • ↑ pH (alkalemia from primary respiratory alkalosis, OR ↓pH late as metabolic acidosis dominates).
  • ↓ PaCO₂ (primary respiratory alkalosis, hyperventilation).
  • ↑ anion gap (metabolic acidosis from accumulated acids).
  • If pH is low + PaCO₂ is low + anion gap high → late mixed pattern.

6. Treatment

Tintinalli Fig 189-1 p.1251
📚 Fig 189-1 — Management algorithm for salicylate poisoning: initial resuscitation → IV access → bicarb bolus → labs → severity-based management (Tintinalli p.1251)

General measures

  1. ABCs — early airway protection if obtunded; volume resuscitation.
  2. IV access × 2; D5NS or NS bolus for volume depletion.
  3. Cardiac monitoring; serial vital signs.
  4. Glucose check — give D50 even with normal serum glucose if AMS (CSF hypoglycemia).

GI Decontamination

  • Activated charcoal 1 g/kg PO — most effective within 1–2 h post-ingestion; can be useful up to 4 h or longer for enteric-coated/large ingestions.
  • Multi-dose activated charcoal may help for very large ingestions (every 2–4 h × 3 doses) — facilitates GI decontamination of bezoars.
  • Whole-bowel irrigation for confirmed pharmacobezoars on imaging or massive enteric-coated ingestion.
  • Airway must be protected.
Pharmacobezoar: aspirin tablets can clump together in the stomach forming a bezoar — delaying absorption and producing peak levels 6–24 h after ingestion. Whole-bowel irrigation + endoscopic removal in extreme cases.

7. Alkalinization (Cornerstone of Treatment)

Tintinalli p.1251

Indications

  • Symptomatic salicylate toxicity (any clinical signs).
  • Salicylate level >30 mg/dL after ingestion.
  • Anion-gap metabolic acidosis.

Sodium bicarbonate dosing

  1. Initial bolus: 1–2 mEq/kg IV (50–100 mEq for 50 kg adult) — to raise serum pH to ~7.45–7.55.
  2. Infusion: 3 ampules NaHCO₃ (150 mEq) in 1 L D5W at 1.5–2× maintenance (about 200–250 mL/h for adult), with potassium 20–40 mEq added per liter (assuming normal K and renal function).
  3. Goal: serum pH 7.50–7.55 AND urine pH ≥7.5 (ideally 7.5–8.5).
  4. Monitor: urine pH q1h, electrolytes (esp. K⁺) q2–4h, ABG q4h, salicylate level q1–2h.

Why hypokalemia matters

  • Alkalinization (and the underlying salicylate-induced hypokalemia) shifts K⁺ intracellularly → urinary K⁺ wasting.
  • Hypokalemia prevents urinary alkalinization — the kidney exchanges H⁺ for K⁺ in the distal tubule when serum K⁺ is low. Even if you give NaHCO₃ IV, urine pH won't rise without normal serum K⁺.
  • Replace aggressively: 20–40 mEq KCl/L of NaHCO₃ infusion + boluses as needed.
⚠️ Pearl — RSI ventilation pitfall: if you intubate a salicylate-toxic patient and set normal ventilator settings, you'll cause sudden, severe acidosis. The patient was hyperventilating (RR 30–40) to compensate; the ventilator at RR 12 will cause CO₂ to rise and pH to crash. Match patient's pre-intubation minute ventilation: RR 25–30 with adequate Vt; consider permissive hyperventilation with continuous bicarbonate infusion. Avoid intubation if possible — get to dialysis instead.

8. Hemodialysis Indications

Tintinalli p.1251

Salicylate is highly dialyzable (low Vd, low protein binding at toxic levels). Hemodialysis removes salicylate efficiently and corrects acid-base abnormalities.

EXTRIP-supported indications for HD in salicylate toxicity

  • Salicylate level >100 mg/dL acute, OR >90 mg/dL with significant acidosis.
  • Salicylate level >60 mg/dL chronic with end-organ toxicity.
  • Severe metabolic acidosis (pH <7.20) refractory to bicarbonate.
  • Altered mental status (any cause attributable to salicylate).
  • Acute lung injury / pulmonary edema requiring O₂.
  • Renal failure (impaired excretion).
  • Hemodynamic instability or fluid overload limiting NaHCO₃ infusion.
  • Need for intubation (intubation with mechanical ventilation usually means hemodialysis is also indicated due to inability to maintain compensatory hyperventilation).
  • Clinical deterioration despite optimal medical therapy.
⚠️ Patients who fail to alkalinize or develop CNS depression need DIALYSIS — not more bicarbonate. Don't wait for "magical numbers" if the patient is clinically declining.

9. Chronic Salicylism / Pediatric Considerations

Tintinalli p.1249–1250

Chronic ("therapeutic") salicylate poisoning

  • Often elderly with chronic pain, arthritis, or aspirin therapy + intercurrent illness/dehydration.
  • Insidious presentation: confusion, weakness, hyperthermia, hyperventilation — often misdiagnosed as sepsis or stroke.
  • Higher mortality than acute single ingestion despite often-lower peak salicylate levels.
  • Lower threshold for dialysis in chronic toxicity (consider HD at salicylate >60 mg/dL with end-organ toxicity).

Reye's syndrome (children)

  • Aspirin given to children with viral illness (especially varicella, influenza) → mitochondrial dysfunction → fatty liver + encephalopathy.
  • This is why aspirin is contraindicated in children <18 with viral illness.

Pediatric salicylate ingestions

  • Often unintentional from oil of wintergreen, BenGay, methyl salicylate.
  • Predominant metabolic acidosis rather than mixed pattern.
  • Lower threshold for treatment — children deteriorate faster.
  • Methyl salicylate teaspoon = potentially lethal in toddler.

10. Disposition

Tintinalli p.1252
  • Discharge: mild ingestion + asymptomatic + downward salicylate trend + normal acid-base + reliable disposition + psychiatric eval (if intentional).
  • Admit telemetry: any toxicity requiring NaHCO₃ infusion; intoxication with co-ingestants.
  • ICU: AMS, severe acidosis, respiratory distress, pulmonary edema, requiring HD, hemodynamic instability, intubated.
  • Continue NaHCO₃ until salicylate <30 mg/dL with normalizing acid-base.
  • Psychiatric consultation for intentional ingestions before discharge.

⚕️ ATLS 11th — Salicylate Considerations

ATLS
  • Suspected salicylate overdose may co-exist with trauma (suicide attempts, polypharmacy, MVCs).
  • Hyperventilation in trauma + AMS — consider salicylate, not just compensation for shock.
  • Avoid intubation if possible — disrupts compensatory hyperventilation. If intubation unavoidable: match patient's pre-intubation minute ventilation (RR 25–30); proceed urgently to HD.
  • Coagulopathy: salicylate inhibits vitamin K-dependent factors → bleeding risk in trauma.
  • Avoid succinylcholine if rhabdomyolysis suspected.
  • Aggressive volume resuscitation with NaHCO₃ in 5% dextrose.

📗 Rosen's EM 10th — Salicylates

Rosen source
Rosen's Ch.146
  • Same general framework — alkalinization + hemodialysis + supportive care.
  • Done nomogram is not recommended per both texts — clinical correlation + serial levels.
  • Activated charcoal up to 4–6 hours for very large or enteric-coated ingestions.
  • Pediatric: earlier hemodialysis threshold and lower toxic dose threshold than adults.
  • Methyl salicylate emphasized — small volumes can be lethal.
  • Some authors recommend NAC for severe salicylate toxicity (analogous mechanism to glutathione precursor) — controversial, evidence weak.
  • Reye's syndrome explanation reinforces the "no aspirin in viral illness in children" recommendation.

🔑 Key Points (High-Yield)

  1. Toxic dose: >150 mg/kg mild, >300 mg/kg severe, >500 mg/kg lethal.
  2. Methyl salicylate (oil of wintergreen) — 5 mL ≈ 21 aspirin tablets; can kill a toddler.
  3. Adult triad: tinnitus + tachypnea/hyperpnea + N/V (early).
  4. Adult acid-base: mixed respiratory alkalosis + AG metabolic acidosis. Children <4 y: predominant metabolic acidosis.
  5. Loss of respiratory alkalosis (rising PaCO₂) = OMINOUS — patient is tiring.
  6. Alkalinization: NaHCO₃ 1–2 mEq/kg bolus, then 150 mEq in 1 L D5W at 1.5–2× maintenance + 20–40 mEq KCl/L. Target serum pH 7.50–7.55, urine pH ≥7.5.
  7. Hypokalemia prevents urinary alkalinization — replace K⁺ aggressively.
  8. Avoid acetazolamide (worsens CNS penetration); avoid intubation if possible.
  9. If intubation unavoidable: match minute ventilation (RR 25–30) + bicarbonate infusion + urgent HD.
  10. Hemodialysis indications: salicylate >100 mg/dL acute / >60 mg/dL chronic, severe acidosis (pH <7.20), AMS, pulmonary edema, renal failure, fluid overload, intubated patient.
  11. Activated charcoal within 1–2 h (4 h for enteric-coated/massive). Multi-dose for bezoars.
  12. D50 empirically in AMS — CSF hypoglycemia with normal serum glucose possible.
  13. Don't use Done nomogram — clinical correlation + serial levels.
  14. Chronic salicylism in elderly = HIGHER mortality at LOWER levels; lower threshold for HD.
  15. Reye's syndrome — aspirin in children with viral illness → mitochondrial dysfunction + fatty liver + encephalopathy. Why aspirin is contraindicated <18 y with viral illness.
