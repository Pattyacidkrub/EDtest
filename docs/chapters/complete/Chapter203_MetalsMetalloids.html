Toxicology Chapter 203

Metals and Metalloids

This chapter is a pattern-recognition chapter: lead is abdominal pain, anemia, neuropathy, encephalopathy, retained bullets, and bone storage; arsenic is severe gastroenteritis, shock, QT/torsades, Mees lines, and neuropathy; mercury is tremor, erethism, acrodynia, corrosive inorganic salt injury, and organic CNS toxicity.

Board lens: the sick patient gets resuscitation and early toxicology consultation. Do not wait for send-out levels when lead encephalopathy or severe acute arsenic poisoning is clinically likely.

Lead

Lead affects the nervous, cardiovascular, hematopoietic, and renal systems. The ED clue is abdominal pain or neurologic dysfunction plus anemia. Blood lead level confirms the diagnosis, but severe clinical toxicity can require treatment before results return.

Tintinalli Tables 203-2 and 203-3
Lead treatment trap: for suspected lead encephalopathy, begin chelation promptly. Dimercaprol is given before/during edetate calcium disodium when CNS symptoms are present.

Arsenic and Arsine

Acute inorganic arsenic produces severe gastroenteritis, capillary leak, hypotension, myocardial dysfunction, QT prolongation, torsades, encephalopathy, ARDS, AKI, and rhabdomyolysis. Subacute toxicity can produce stocking-glove neuropathy and Mees lines after several weeks.

Tintinalli Tables 203-4 and 203-5
Arsine gas: inhalation causes intravascular hemolysis and renal failure, not classic acute arsenic poisoning. Treat supportively with transfusion/exchange transfusion and dialysis as needed; chelation has no role.

Mercury

ElementalVapor inhalation is dangerous; intact GI absorption after swallowing is usually negligible.
InorganicCorrosive gastroenteritis, gray oral mucosa, shock, AKI; salts do not enter CNS well.
OrganicLatent weeks-months; orofacial paresthesias, ataxia, tremor, blindness, hearing deficits, dementia.
Chronic cluesTremor, neurasthenia, erethism, acrodynia/pink disease.
Tintinalli Table 203-6

Other Metals

Less common metals often begin with nonspecific GI irritation followed by neurologic, renal, hematologic, or cutaneous injury. Thallium is especially testable: early GI symptoms, painful ascending neuropathy, dysrhythmias/AMS, then delayed alopecia. Metal fume fever causes fever, chills, body aches, headache, and fatigue after inhaling zinc/aluminum/magnesium oxide fumes.
