1. Introduction

Tintinalli Ch.187 p.1238

Rosen vs Tintinalli: Rosen adds ED-facing nuance for Cocaine and amphetamines while keeping the Tintinalli treatment spine.

  • Cocaine — alkaloid from Erythroxylum coca; first used as topical anesthetic (1884) and now a major drug of abuse worldwide.
  • Amphetamines — broad class including methamphetamine, MDMA, methylphenidate, ephedrine, and "designer" cathinones ("bath salts").
  • Both produce a sympathomimetic toxidrome: HTN, tachycardia, hyperthermia, mydriasis, diaphoresis, agitation/psychosis.
  • Most ED visits driven by chest pain, agitation, seizures, or trauma related to use.

2. Pharmacology & Pharmacokinetics

Tintinalli Tables 187-1, 187-2, 187-3

Cocaine

  • Mechanism: blocks reuptake of norepinephrine, dopamine, serotonin → sympathomimetic effects. Also blocks fast Na⁺ channels (local anesthetic / type 1A antiarrhythmic effect).
  • Forms: hydrochloride salt (powder for snorting/IV); freebase ("crack") for smoking — melts at lower temperature.
  • Major metabolite: benzoylecgonine — used for urine drug screening. Detection up to 2 weeks in heavy users.
  • Half-life: ~1 hour parent compound; benzoylecgonine ~6 hours.

Methamphetamine & Amphetamines

  • Mechanism: increases release of catecholamines (vs cocaine's reuptake block); inhibits MAO; long duration of action.
  • Half-life: methamphetamine ~10 hours (much longer than cocaine).
  • Methylenedioxy derivatives (MDMA, MDA) have additional serotonergic effect → risk of serotonin syndrome and hyponatremia.

4. Cardiovascular Toxicity

Tintinalli p.1239
  • Coronary vasoconstriction (α-mediated) → demand-supply mismatch → ischemia/MI.
  • Accelerated atherosclerosis in chronic users.
  • Pro-thrombotic state — platelet activation, ↑ fibrinogen.
  • Dysrhythmias: sinus tachycardia most common; supraventricular tachycardia, wide-complex tachycardia from Na-channel blockade, VT/VF.
  • Aortic dissection — strongly associated with cocaine use even at younger ages.
  • Cardiomyopathy — both acute (stress/Takotsubo-like) and chronic.
  • Endocarditis in IV users.
⚠️ Cocaine-associated chest pain risk: 6% MI rate when patients with cocaine-related chest pain are evaluated. Q-wave MI risk peaks within 60 min of use, but elevated risk continues for ≥24 h. Younger patients with no traditional CV risk factors may still infarct.

5. CNS Toxicity

Tintinalli p.1239–1240
  • Seizures — generalized or focal; often related to vasospasm or hyperthermia. New-onset seizure post-cocaine warrants imaging.
  • Cerebral infarction — vasoconstriction; embolic from endocarditis; hemorrhagic from sustained HTN.
  • Subarachnoid/intracerebral hemorrhage — rupture of pre-existing AVM/aneurysm precipitated by HTN surge.
  • Movement disorders — choreoathetosis ("crack dancing") in heavy chronic use.
  • Excited delirium / agitated delirium — extreme agitation, hyperthermia, sudden death; high-acuity emergency.
⚠️ Excited delirium syndrome: Highly agitated, hyperthermic patient who collapses suddenly during restraint. Aggressive sedation (benzos ± ketamine), evaporative cooling, IV fluids, and ECG monitoring save lives. Avoid prolonged physical restraint.

6. Other Systemic Toxicity

Tintinalli p.1240

Pulmonary

  • Crack lung — diffuse alveolar damage; cough, hemoptysis, hypoxemia.
  • Barotrauma from Valsalva during inhalation — pneumomediastinum, pneumothorax.
  • Asthma exacerbation; pulmonary hemorrhage; talc granulomatosis (IV).

GI

  • Mesenteric ischemia from vasoconstriction; ulcers.
  • Body packers (intentional concealment, well-wrapped) and body stuffers (panic ingestion, often unwrapped) — risk of perforation/leak.

Renal

  • Rhabdomyolysis from agitation, hyperthermia, seizures → AKI.
  • Renal infarction from vasoconstriction.

Pregnancy

  • Placental abruption, preterm labor, IUGR, spontaneous abortion.

Hyperthermia

  • Excessive muscle activity + impaired thermoregulation → core temp may exceed 40°C.
  • Aggressive evaporative or immersion cooling required; aim for <39°C rapidly.

7. Diagnosis & Differential

Tintinalli Table 187-4

Clinical diagnosis based on history, vital signs, and toxidrome. Lab/imaging directed by clinical picture.

  • Urine immunoassay: cocaine metabolite (benzoylecgonine) — detects cocaine well; designer amphetamines and bath salts often miss.
  • Confirmation: GC/MS or LC/MS/MS.
  • Other labs: ECG, CK, troponin, electrolytes (esp. K⁺, Na⁺ for MDMA), creatinine, ABG/lactate, CT head if AMS or focal neuro.

8. Treatment

Tintinalli Table 187-5 p.1241
📚 Table 187-5 — Management of Sympathomimetic Toxicity (Tintinalli p.1241)

Sedation — first-line

  • Benzodiazepines (lorazepam 2 mg IV, diazepam 5 mg IV, midazolam 5 mg IM) — titrate to calm, no maximum dose.
  • Antipsychotics (haloperidol, droperidol) — second-line; caution re QT prolongation.
  • Ketamine (4 mg/kg IM) — for severe excited delirium.

Hyperthermia

  • Aggressive cooling (target temp <39°C); evaporative / ice bath / endovascular catheters.
  • Sedation reduces heat production from agitation.

Hypertension

  • Benzodiazepines first.
  • Phentolamine 1–5 mg IV (α-blocker) for refractory HTN — ideal because it doesn't worsen "unopposed α".
  • Nitroglycerin for ongoing chest pain.
  • Avoid pure β-blockers (theoretical concern); labetalol is debated — safe per some guidelines but not universally accepted as first-line.

Dysrhythmias

  • Wide-complex tachycardia (Na-channel block by cocaine): sodium bicarbonate 1–2 mEq/kg IV bolus.
  • Refractory VT: lidocaine.
  • SVT: benzo first, then standard ACLS if persistent.

Seizures

  • Benzodiazepines first; phenytoin/phenobarbital second.
  • If status, intubate + propofol/midazolam infusion.

Body Packers / Stuffers

  • Stuffer (panic ingestion): activated charcoal if recent; observe.
  • Packer (intentional, well-wrapped): whole-bowel irrigation with PEG; surgery if leak/obstruction.
  • NEVER attempt endoscopic retrieval — risk of bag rupture.

9. Cocaine-Associated Chest Pain

Tintinalli p.1241

Approach mirrors standard MI workup with key modifications:

  1. ECG — interpret cautiously; cocaine often causes early-repolarization ST elevation that is benign.
  2. Troponin — elevated indicates true MI, not just toxic effect.
  3. Initial therapy: oxygen if hypoxic, aspirin, IV benzodiazepines, nitroglycerin.
  4. Heparin / antiplatelet: per standard ACS algorithm if MI confirmed.
  5. PCI if STEMI; thrombolytics carry slightly higher hemorrhage risk in cocaine users (sympathetic-driven HTN).
  6. β-blockers — controversial; AHA gives Class IIb recommendation for those with confirmed cocaine MI after management of acute toxicity. Most prefer to wait until cocaine effects resolve (~24 h) before initiating.
  7. Disposition: low-risk patients (negative ECG/troponin × 9–12 h, resolved symptoms, no high-risk features) may be discharged. The HEART pathway has been validated for cocaine chest pain.
"Unopposed α" debate: classical teaching suggests pure β-blockers worsen cocaine-induced vasoconstriction by leaving α-effects unopposed. Recent evidence is mixed; labetalol (mixed α/β) is widely used but not universally endorsed. Default position: avoid pure β-blockers acutely; use phentolamine or labetalol if necessary; benzos remain first-line.

10. Disposition

Tintinalli p.1241
  • Discharge: brief intoxication, mild HTN/tachycardia, normal ECG, normal mental status after observation (typically 4–6 h).
  • Admit: persistent abnormal vitals, end-organ damage (MI, stroke, AKI, rhabdomyolysis), severe hyperthermia, respiratory complications, body packers/stuffers awaiting clearance.
  • ICU: excited delirium, refractory hyperthermia, seizures requiring intubation, dysrhythmias, hemodynamic instability.

⚕️ ATLS 11th — Stimulant Intoxication in Trauma

ATLS Ch. on Toxicology / Special Populations

Stimulant-intoxicated trauma patients pose specific challenges:

  • Cooperative assessment may be impossible — agitation impedes ABCDE survey.
  • Heart rate and BP may be elevated independently of hemorrhage — easy to underestimate shock.
  • Rhabdomyolysis from agitation/restraints in addition to crush — early IVF, urine output monitoring.
  • Aortic dissection in cocaine users — consider in chest/back pain even without classic risk factors.
  • Sedation: use benzodiazepines/ketamine; avoid succinylcholine in known hyperkalemia from rhabdo.
  • Imaging: low threshold for CT chest (dissection), CT head (hemorrhage).

📗 Rosen's EM 10th — Cocaine and Amphetamines

Rosen source
Rosen's Ch.149
  • Same general approach as Tintinalli: benzo-first, treat hyperthermia aggressively, sodium bicarbonate for wide-QRS.
  • Rosen's emphasizes: cocaine-induced cardiac ischemia mechanisms are multifactorial (vasospasm, increased demand, prothrombotic, accelerated atherosclerosis).
  • HEART score / pathway validated for cocaine-related chest pain — most low-risk patients can be safely discharged after 9–12 h.
  • Highlights danger of "crack lung" presentation requiring high-flow O₂ and steroids in some cases.
  • Rosen's also covers chronic complications: cardiomyopathy, cerebrovascular disease, gastroduodenal perforation, nasal septal perforation in chronic snorters.

🔑 Key Points (High-Yield)

  1. Sympathomimetic toxidrome: HTN + tachycardia + hyperthermia + diaphoresis + mydriasis + agitation. Diaphoresis distinguishes from anticholinergic.
  2. Cocaine blocks Na⁺ channels (type IA effect) → wide QRS responds to sodium bicarbonate.
  3. Amphetamine mechanism = release + reuptake inhibition + MAO inhibition; longer half-life (~10 h vs ~1 h cocaine).
  4. Bath salts miss standard urine immunoassays — need GC/MS.
  5. MDMA + free water → severe hyponatremia + cerebral edema (treat with 3% saline).
  6. Cocaine chest pain: benzo + nitroglycerin + ASA first; β-blockers controversial. Phentolamine for refractory HTN.
  7. Hyperthermia in stimulants = muscle hyperactivity; cool aggressively + benzodiazepines. Antipyretics ineffective.
  8. Excited delirium — sedate aggressively (ketamine ± benzos); avoid prolonged restraint.
  9. Body packers (wrapped, large) → WBI/PEG; stuffers (unwrapped, smaller) → activated charcoal.
  10. Aortic dissection in young cocaine user with chest/back pain — high suspicion.
  11. Pregnancy + cocaine = abruption, preterm labor, IUGR.
  12. Disposition for cocaine chest pain: 9–12 h observation acceptable if low risk.
