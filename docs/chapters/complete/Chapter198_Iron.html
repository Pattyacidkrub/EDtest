Toxicology Chapter 198

Iron Poisoning

Iron is a deceptively common household toxin: prenatal vitamins, adult supplements, pediatric vitamins, and industrial formulations. The exam hinge is not “did the patient take iron?” but elemental ionic iron dose, early vomiting, acidosis/shock, and whether deferoxamine is needed.

Board lens: absence of GI symptoms within 6 hours essentially excludes significant ingestion, but a “latent” period after initial vomiting can falsely reassure before systemic toxicity declares itself.

Overview

Most consistent clinical signVomiting in the first few hours after significant ingestion.
Danger doseModerate toxicity: 20-60 mg/kg elemental ionic iron. Severe toxicity: >60 mg/kg.
Rule-out clueNo GI symptoms within 6 hours makes significant acute ingestion unlikely.
Do not anchor on level aloneSerum iron is timing-dependent and can be artificially low after deferoxamine.

Pharmacology and Formulations

Normal physiology keeps iron bound to transferrin, ferritin, hemoglobin, myoglobin, cytochromes, and other proteins. In overdose, binding and mucosal regulation are overwhelmed; free iron can then injure gut mucosa, mitochondria, liver, vasculature, myocardium, and coagulation pathways.

Toxicity Thresholds

Systemic toxicity pattern: shock, lactic acidosis, coagulopathy, renal failure, cardiomyopathy, and later hepatic failure. Severe disease is a physiology diagnosis.

Clinical Features

Stage 1: GIAbdominal pain, vomiting, diarrhea, mucosal injury, bleeding.
Stage 2: latent6-24 h. Symptoms may improve while systemic toxicity evolves.
Stage 3: shock/acidosisIron disrupts cellular metabolism; coagulopathy may be early and later hepatic.
Stage 4: hepatic2-5 days: transaminase rise and possible fulminant hepatic failure.
Stage 5: delayed obstruction4-6 weeks: rare gastric outlet obstruction from pyloric injury.

Diagnosis

Iron toxicity is largely a clinical diagnosis. Labs assess severity: CBC, electrolytes/anion gap, renal and liver function, coagulation, glucose, and serum iron. ABG/lactate are most useful in moderate/severe disease or respiratory compromise.

Imaging Source Check

Radiopaque tablets can guide decontamination, but a negative film does not exclude ingestion because many preparations are not visible.

Key Points

1. Calculate elemental ironFerrous sulfate 325 mg = 65 mg elemental iron.
2. Vomiting mattersMost consistent sign of significant acute toxicity.
3. No GI symptoms by 6 h reassuresEssentially excludes significant ingestion.
4. Do not trust charcoalIt does not bind meaningful iron.
5. Radiograph helps, but negative does not rule outSome formulations are radiolucent.
6. Treat the patient, not the level aloneDeferoxamine can lower measured serum iron artifactually.
7. Deferoxamine is IV in practiceStart slow to avoid hypotension.
8. Watch late liver failureRapid transaminase rise + coagulopathy + AKI needs transplant-level thinking.
