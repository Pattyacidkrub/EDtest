High-risk cyanotic congenital heart disease

1  Clinical Pathway From recognition to disposition
1

Presentation

  • Sudden or episodic central cyanosis
  • Irritability, inconsolable crying, agitation
  • Dyspnea, tachypnea, poor feeding
  • Squatting in older children
  • Syncope or near syncope in severe spells
2

Initial Assessment

  • ABCs, continuous monitoring, IV/IO access
  • Vitals: SpO2, HR, BP, RR, temperature
  • Central vs acral cyanosis; perfusion and pulses
  • Focused cardiac exam: single S2, systolic ejection murmur at mid/lower LSB, thrill
  • Look for triggers: crying, fever, dehydration, anemia, pain, agitation
3

Is This a Tet Spell?

Hypercyanotic crisis
  • Known or suspected TOF
  • Acute worsening cyanosis; SpO2 often <80%-85%
  • Agitation, lethargy, or syncope
  • No better pulmonary or toxicologic explanation
YES NO
If no, evaluate sepsis, pneumonia/asthma, foreign body, methemoglobinemia, pulmonary hypertension, and other cyanotic CHD.
4

Immediate Maneuvers

  • Call pediatric cardiology and ICU early
  • Parent's arms, calm room, minimize stimulation
  • Knee-chest position or squatting posture
  • 100% oxygen by nonrebreather mask
  • Treat fever, pain, dehydration, anemia, acidosis
5

Medications

Use the dose ladder in the green column. Escalate if cyanosis, distress, or acidosis persists.

6

Response Evaluation

  • Improved color, mental status, and respiratory effort
  • SpO2 returns toward known baseline
  • If no improvement: pressor/beta-blocker escalation, bicarbonate, ketamine, and airway plan
7

Disposition

  • Admit to ICU; continuous SpO2, ECG, BP monitoring
  • Cardiology consultation and echocardiography
  • Correct precipitating factors
  • Definitive repair is surgical; timing per pediatric cardiology/cardiac surgery
Tetralogy of Fallot - Pathophysiology
1Large VSDNonrestrictive ventricular septal defect equalizes RV and LV systolic pressures.
2RVOT obstructionValvular, supravalvular, or infundibular pulmonary stenosis limits pulmonary blood flow.
3Overriding aortaAorta receives blood from both ventricles across the septal defect.
4RV hypertrophyChronic pressure load from outflow obstruction thickens the right ventricle.
Tet Spell Mechanism: Vicious Cycle
Increased RVOT obstruction Low preload, low SVR, infundibular spasm, agitation, acidosis
↓

↓
Increased right-to-left shunt across VSD
↓

↓

↓
Worse RVOT obstruction + R-to-L shunt = worsening hypoxemia

  • Decreased pulmonary vascular markings

ECG

Physiology Pearls

  • Severity of cyanosis tracks with the degree of RVOT obstruction.
  • Knee-chest increases venous return and SVR, reducing R-to-L shunt.
  • Oxygen treats hypoxia and promotes pulmonary vasodilation, but cyanotic CHD may not fully correct with oxygen.
  • Acyanotic or "pink" TOF can occur when pulmonary stenosis is mild and shunt is left-to-right.

Refractory / Critical

  • Persistent cyanosis or SpO2 below baseline despite treatment
  • Altered mental status, syncope, seizure, or severe agitation
  • Hypotension, bradycardia, severe acidosis
  • Prepare for pressors, intubation, neuromuscular blockade, and urgent cardiology evaluation

Undiagnosed Cyanotic Neonate

  • If cyanotic heart disease cannot be ruled out: prostaglandin E1 infusion 0.05 mcg/kg/min, titrate to minimum effective dose.
  • Chapter 129 table lists 0.05-0.1 mcg/kg/min in shock with abnormal cardiac exam in infants <1 month.
  • Caution: obstructed TAPVR and PPHN require expert guidance.

Differential Diagnosis

  • Breath-holding spells
  • Sepsis or severe pneumonia
  • Asthma/status asthmaticus
  • Foreign body aspiration
  • Methemoglobinemia
  • Transposition, TAPVR, tricuspid atresia, truncus arteriosus

Key Points to Remember

  • Tet spell is acute R-to-L shunting through VSD, usually from worsened RVOT obstruction.
  • Treat physiology: increase preload, increase SVR, reduce RVOT obstruction, reduce PVR, correct acidosis.
  • Hyperoxia test: poor PaO2 rise with 100% oxygen supports cardiac mixing or R-to-L shunt.
  • Early cardiology involvement improves safety; definitive therapy is surgical repair.
